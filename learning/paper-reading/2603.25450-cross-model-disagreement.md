> Paper: [arXiv:2603.25450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25450)<br>
> Reading status: **Read** · Relevance: **High**

## One-sentence summary

The paper uses a verifier model's token-level surprise toward another model's generated answer as a label-free correctness signal, addressing confident errors that are difficult to detect using the generator's own uncertainty.

## Research question

Can we determine whether a generated answer is likely to be correct without ground-truth labels, additional training, or asking the verifier model to generate another answer?

## Method

Given an answer produced by a sample model, the verifier performs a single teacher-forced forward pass over that answer. The paper defines two disagreement signals:

1. **Cross-Model Perplexity (CMP):** how surprising the sampled answer tokens are under the verifier model.
2. **Cross-Model Entropy (CME):** how uncertain the verifier is at the corresponding token positions.

The verifier does not need to generate text. The scores can therefore be used in monitoring, routing, selective prediction, or data filtering pipelines.

## Key findings

### Finding 1: cross-model signals outperform self-uncertainty baselines

- **Claim:** CMP and CME distinguish correct and incorrect answers better than uncertainty computed only from the generating model.
- **Evidence:** The paper reports improvements across MMLU, TriviaQA, and GSM8K.
- **Condition:** The answer is generated by one model and scored through a forward pass by another model.
- **Confidence:** High; this is a central empirical claim of the paper.

### Finding 2: disagreement remains useful for confident errors

- **Claim:** A generator can assign high confidence to an incorrect answer, while a different model may still find its token sequence surprising.
- **Why it matters:** This targets the failure mode in which entropy or confidence from the sample model itself appears normal despite an incorrect result.
- **Confidence:** High; this is the main motivation for using cross-model rather than within-model uncertainty.

### Finding 3: the reported gain is quantitatively meaningful

- **Evidence:** On MMLU, CMP reaches a mean AUROC of **0.75**, compared with **0.59** for a within-model entropy baseline.
- **Caveat:** Performance should still be checked for each generator-verifier pair and domain.

## My insights

### Model independence may be part of the signal

The useful disagreement may not come only from one model being "stronger." It may depend on the two models having sufficiently different error modes. Two independently trained models can disagree on semantically fragile or incorrect trajectories, while closely related models may share most token preferences and failure patterns.

### Connection to RL model versus base model

In my experiments, JS divergence between an independently selected sample model and judge model separates correct and incorrect samples, but divergence between an RL model and its own base model is much weaker. A plausible explanation is that the RL model and base model are highly correlated: RL often changes a small number of decision tokens, reorders probabilities among top candidates, and sharpens the distribution without creating an independent representation of correctness.

This is **my hypothesis**, not a conclusion established by this paper. It suggests testing whether correctness separability is controlled by model independence, training lineage, or verifier diversity.

## Limitations and open questions

- How sensitive are CMP and CME to verifier size and capability?
- Does the signal disappear when the verifier and generator share the same initialization or training lineage?
- Is disagreement concentrated at decision tokens, or distributed across stylistic and formatting tokens?
- How much of the result comes from semantic disagreement versus surface-form likelihood?
- Can token-level disagreement be calibrated across responses of different lengths?

## Follow-up experiments

- [ ] Compare independent model pairs with RL/base model pairs while controlling for model size.
- [ ] Measure JS divergence only at high-impact decision tokens.
- [ ] Separate probability sharpening from top-$k$ token reordering.
- [ ] Evaluate answer-level AUROC with and without length normalization.
- [ ] Group samples by correctness and plot where disagreement appears in the reasoning trace.
